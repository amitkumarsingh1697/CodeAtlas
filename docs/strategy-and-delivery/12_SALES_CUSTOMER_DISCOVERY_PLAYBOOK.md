# Sales & Customer Discovery Playbook

## 1. ICP
Boutique/mid-sized modernization consultancies and software engineering firms repeatedly assessing unfamiliar client codebases, with source confidentiality constraints.

## 2. Discovery questions
Ask about the last real project:
1. Tell me about the last unfamiliar or inherited codebase your team had to understand.
2. What triggered the assessment?
3. How long did discovery take and who was involved?
4. What deliverables did the client expect?
5. Which parts were manual?
6. What was missed or discovered late?
7. What tools did you use?
8. What source-code handling restrictions did the client impose?
9. Who approved the budget?
10. What would make a new assessment tool acceptable?
11. What is the cost of the current process?
12. Can we scope a paid assessment against a real project?

Avoid “Would you use this?” Ask about behavior, budgets and past decisions.

## 3. Qualification
Qualify pain, urgency, repository access authorization, stack fit, buyer, budget, procurement, security requirements and decision timeline.

## 4. Demo sequence (15–20 minutes)
1. Start with customer problem and scope.
2. Show local execution and data-flow statement.
3. Show technology inventory and coverage.
4. Search a real symbol/table.
5. Inspect observed vs inferred relationships.
6. Show source evidence.
7. Show impact analysis and limitations.
8. Show sample report.
9. Ask what would be useful in their current engagement.
10. Propose bounded paid pilot.

## 5. Outreach
Personalized email/LinkedIn message should reference the consultancy's modernization or legacy work, state local-first analysis and propose a short discovery call. Do not claim guaranteed accuracy, universal language coverage or compliance certification.

## 6. Funnel hypotheses
100 named target accounts → 30 meaningful replies/conversations → 10 qualified → 5 scoped proposals → 1–3 paid pilots. These are planning assumptions, not expected conversion benchmarks.

## 7. CRM fields
Account, country, segment, contact, role, source, last touch, pain, stack, repository size, confidentiality constraints, current process, budget, buyer, urgency, next action, proposal value, outcome, objection, follow-on potential.

## 8. Customer success
Agree success criteria before scan. After delivery, ask:
- Which findings were used?
- What did we get wrong?
- What did we miss?
- Did the report change a decision?
- How much time did it save or cost?
- Would you pay to repeat or continuously use it?
- Can we obtain a written reference/case study with approved redactions?
