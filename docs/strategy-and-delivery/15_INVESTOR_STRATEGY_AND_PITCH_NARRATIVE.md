# Investor Strategy & Pitch Narrative

## 1. Investor thesis
CodeAtlas aims to become a trusted software architecture intelligence layer for organizations that need to understand and change complex existing software. Its entry wedge is local-first, evidence-backed architecture assessment for modernization consultancies. Its expansion path is recurring repository intelligence, change-impact analysis and enterprise governance.

## 2. Pitch deck storyline
1. Title: CodeAtlas — Local-first Software Architecture Intelligence.
2. Problem: understanding inherited software is slow, fragmented and risky.
3. Why now: software estates are changing faster, AI-assisted development is increasing change velocity, and security constraints remain.
4. Solution: local analysis, evidence-labelled graph, coverage and client-ready assessment.
5. Product demo: scan → map → inspect evidence → impact → report.
6. Differentiation: evidence semantics, local execution, cross-layer mapping, assessment workflow.
7. Market: define bottom-up reachable customer segments; do not use adjacent AI coding-agent market as CodeAtlas TAM.
8. Business model: paid assessments → Professional → Enterprise.
9. Go-to-market: founder-led India and international boutique consultancy outreach.
10. Competition: GitNexus, Swimm, CAST, Sourcegraph, SciTools and native coding tools; compare workflows and evidence, not superficial feature counts.
11. Traction: actual paid pilots, revenue, conversion, repeat use, time saved, accuracy benchmark.
12. Roadmap: stack accuracy, reports, packaging, recurring product, enterprise.
13. Team: founder's relevant technical experience and execution capacity; add only verified bios.
14. Financials and funding ask: only after costs, traction and hiring plan are validated.

## 3. Do not invent
- TAM/SAM/SOM without a transparent bottom-up method.
- Customer logos, testimonials, pipeline or revenue.
- Accuracy percentages.
- Productivity/time-saved metrics.
- Market share or competitor weakness.
- Funding ask or valuation without a use-of-funds and runway model.
- Founder/team credentials not verified.

## 4. Seed readiness evidence
- 3–5+ paying customers or equivalent strong evidence (not a universal threshold).
- Repeat usage and expansion signals.
- Customer references and case studies with permission.
- Stack-specific accuracy benchmark.
- Secure and repeatable deployment.
- Clear IP ownership and open-source compliance.
- Cohort/retention or repeat-purchase evidence.
- Unit economics and gross margin.
- Defined use of funds tied to measurable milestones.

## 5. Funding strategy
Do not raise solely to finish a feature list. Raise when capital accelerates a validated motion: engineering coverage, distribution, enterprise deployment or customer acquisition. Model 18–24 months of runway as a planning scenario, then adjust for market and investor expectations.

## 6. Investor objections to prepare
- Why can't Claude Code/Cursor do this?
- Why not GitNexus, CAST, Swimm or SciTools?
- How accurate is it on real enterprise systems?
- What data leaves the customer's environment?
- What is the recurring use case?
- Who owns the budget?
- What is the sales cycle?
- What does delivery cost?
- How does open-core monetize?
- What prevents a fork or platform feature?
- Why this founder/team?
