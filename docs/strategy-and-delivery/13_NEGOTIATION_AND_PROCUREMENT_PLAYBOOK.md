# Negotiation & Procurement Playbook

## Principles
Sell outcomes, not hours or feature counts. Protect scope and margin. Never negotiate away security, evidence integrity or honest limitations. Do not promise features, certifications or delivery dates that are not verified.

## Common objections
### “We cannot share source code.”
Response: “The default assessment is designed for customer-controlled local execution. Your team selects the repository and runs the analysis. We can review approved outputs together. We will document exactly what is processed and whether anything leaves your environment.”

### “We already use Claude Code/Cursor/GitNexus.”
Response: “Those tools may be sufficient for some workflows. Our proposed assessment focuses on a repeatable architecture inventory, explicit relationship evidence, coverage and a client-ready report. We should compare on one bounded task rather than claim superiority.”

### “How accurate is it?”
Response: “We publish results by stack and relationship type, including unsupported and unresolved cases. We do not claim complete static understanding. We can agree the exact capability scope before the pilot.”

### “Can you guarantee no missed dependencies?”
Response: “No static analysis tool should promise that. We report known coverage, observed and inferred relationships, unresolved areas and framework limitations.”

### “Can you reduce the price?”
Trade scope, not just price: fewer modules, one report format, fewer workshops, longer turnaround, limited support. Never deliver the full scope at a discounted price without documenting the concession.

### “We need on-prem/air-gapped.”
Confirm exact environment, architecture, outbound restrictions, installation, support and update requirements. Treat custom deployment as separately scoped and priced.

## Negotiation levers
- Scope and repository count.
- Delivery schedule.
- Workshop hours.
- Support period.
- Report customization.
- Payment schedule.
- Reference/case-study permission (never make confidentiality a condition unless explicitly negotiated).
- Pilot-to-annual credit.

## Contract essentials
Statement of work, scope, exclusions, customer authorization, customer data ownership, confidentiality, security responsibilities, deliverables, acceptance criteria, payment milestones, change requests, liability limits, warranty disclaimer, IP ownership, licence terms, retention/deletion, support, termination, governing law and dispute resolution. Obtain legal review.

## Procurement pack
- Product overview.
- Deployment/data-flow diagram.
- Security questionnaire.
- Threat model summary.
- SBOM and release integrity information when available.
- Data processing terms if applicable.
- Privacy and retention statement.
- Vulnerability disclosure contact.
- Support and incident response process.
- Licence and third-party notices.
